# 讲稿

**两个半场，各约 300 词、约 2 分 20 秒。** 当天抽到谁，谁挑一个半场。两半互不依赖，顺序固定 A→B。

> 读法：**加粗**的词重读并停半拍。`[ ]` 里是动作提示，不念出来。
> 语速按每分钟 130 词算过。讲不完就砍掉标了 *(可删)* 的句子。

---

## 半场 A — 问题与一个答案

### 页 1 · The night the grid fell over （0:40）

> Twenty-eighth of September, two-thousand-sixteen. South Australia.
>
> In one instant, the state lost about **one thousand megawatts** of supply. That was **fifty-five percent** of everything it was using.
>
> `[停半拍]`
>
> The grid has an automatic defence for this: it switches customers off on purpose, to save the rest. That defence needs **a hundred and fifty milliseconds** to act.
>
> It never got them. Frequency fell through its whole operating range faster than the protection could respond, and the state went dark.
>
> So: that blackout was not a shortage of energy. It was a shortage of **speed**.

### 页 2 · Frequency is the error signal （0:45）

> Why does speed matter? Because electricity is almost impossible to store. Whatever the grid generates right now, it must use right now.
>
> When those two don't match, the difference goes into the spinning generators. Too little generation, they slow down. And their speed **is** the grid frequency.
>
> `[指框图]`
>
> So the grid is a closed loop — and **frequency is the error signal**. Fifty hertz is the setpoint. If frequency drops, generation is losing.
>
> One more piece. Those machines are heavy. Their stored motion holds the frequency up for a few seconds. That's **inertia**, and it's free — nobody controls it, it just happens.
>
> Wind and solar connect through power electronics. They have almost none to give. *(可删: So the more renewables you add, the less time your controller gets.)*

### 页 3 · A faster controller （0:55）

> South Australia's answer was a battery — the Hornsdale Power Reserve. **A hundred megawatts**, a hundred and twenty-nine megawatt-hours.
>
> But the number that matters isn't the size. It responds in about **a hundred milliseconds**.
>
> `[停半拍]`
>
> The market it bid into required six seconds. Hornsdale is roughly **sixty times faster** than anyone asked for.
>
> And it paid. An independent study by Aurecon found that in two-thousand-nineteen it cut costs across the Australian market by about **a hundred and sixteen million dollars**. In South Australia the price of frequency regulation fell **ninety-one percent**.
>
> `[交接]` That's the problem, and that's one answer. But it isn't quite that simple — and that's where my colleague picks up.

## 半场 B — 为什么不能靠一个器件，以及这件事的意义

### 页 4 · One device can't do both （0:50）

> So if speed is what we need, why not just build faster batteries and stop there?
>
> Because **energy and power are not the same thing**.
>
> `[用水桶比喻，配合手势]`
>
> Think of a bucket. Energy is how much water is in it. Power is how wide the tap is. A big bucket with a narrow tap holds a lot but pours slowly. A small bucket with a wide tap empties in seconds.
>
> A lithium battery is the big bucket. A **supercapacitor** is the wide tap. `[指表]`
>
> Per kilogram, a supercapacitor stores **twenty to forty times less** energy than a lithium cell. But it delivers power about **a hundred times faster**. And it survives around **a million** charge cycles, where the battery manages around a thousand.
>
> That last number is the one engineers care about. Asking a battery to absorb every fast fluctuation doesn't just strain it — it spends its life.

### 页 5 · So you split the job （0:50）

> Which gives you the design. You don't choose. You use **both**, and you split the work **by timescale**.
>
> `[指示意图]` The fast part of the demand goes to the capacitor. The slow part goes to the battery. In control terms it can be as simple as a filter.
>
> And this is not a classroom idea. In **August twenty-twenty-five**, a hundred-megawatt plant connected to the grid in Xinzhou, in Shanxi province. Forty-two megawatts of lithium battery, and **fifty-eight megawatts of supercapacitors**.
>
> Its energy management system does exactly what I just described: **millisecond** events go to the supercapacitor, **minute-scale** regulation goes to the battery.
>
> More supercapacitor than battery, by power. For a job that is all about speed, that's the right way round.

### 页 6 · Control is replacing physics （0:50）

> I want to finish with the part we found genuinely surprising.
>
> Britain's grid has a minimum amount of inertia it insists on holding — that free buffer from the spinning machines. In two-thousand-twenty-two the floor was **one hundred and forty** gigavolt-ampere-seconds. It was then cut to **one hundred and twenty**. And the regulator has now proposed cutting it again, to **one hundred and two**.
>
> `[停半拍]`
>
> They are not losing that buffer by accident. They are **choosing to need less of it** — because fast closed-loop control has become good enough to do the job that spinning steel used to do.
>
> That's the whole story in one line. A control system is replacing a physical property of the machine. `[停]`
>
> Thank you.

## 千万别做的事

- **别念幻灯片上的字。** 幻灯片是给听众看的，讲稿是给耳朵的，两者不重叠。
- **别加"我们认为""我觉得"。** 每个数字都有出处，直接陈述。
- **别把 MW·s 念成 MW。** 页 6 那个单位是"吉伏安秒"，不是功率。
- **别道歉。** 不说 "sorry my English"、不说 "I'm not sure but"。讲错了就继续往下。
